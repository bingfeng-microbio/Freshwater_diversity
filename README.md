# Global hotspots of microbiota for lake ecological conservation


The scripts used in the study

Microbes represent the largest portion of biodiversity in lakes, and provide key services such as clean water and pathogen control for humankind. The fate of this unique biodiversity remains largely unknown in the context of global change. Here, we integrate 1,597 metagenomic lake samples with machine-learning models to systematically evaluate the extent to which the structure and function of lake microbiomes (archaea, bacteria, fungi, and viruses) are affected by global change. This knowledge is key to determining the global conservation priorities for lake microbiota and functions. Our work reveals that conserving lake microbial diversity and function is challenging as hotspots for different ecological dimensions (alpha diversity, community dissimilarity, and functional potential) are found across different lakes worldwide. Our projections reveal that fossil fuel-dependent future scenarios will lead 56.9% of global lakes to experience strong declines in microbial diversity or functional potential (i.e., belonging to the highest category of projected decreases in our data-driven change classification). We identified the hotspot priorities for lake conservation, and found that only 13.4% of such lakes are currently protected. Our work provides a robust framework and identifies key priorities for integrating conservation strategies with targeted climate intervention policies for managing microbial hotspots in lakes worldwide and helps identify conservation priorities.
